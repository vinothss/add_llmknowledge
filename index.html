    Adding Knowledge to LLMs

    A self-paced, hands-on workshop. You'll take a general-purpose LLM, teach it the products, policies and safety rules of a company it has never heard of, and make it small enough to deploy.

    Every lab runs in Google Colab: nothing to install and no GPU of your own. The free T4 GPU covers the whole core path. Colab Pro unlocks bonus exercises with bigger models.

    Meet Lumora Energy

    All five modules follow one company: Lumora Energy. It's fictional, invented for this workshop, so no model has ever seen anything about it. That makes it a clean test: whatever the model knows about Lumora at the end, you taught it.

    Lumora was founded in 2017 in Rotterdam and makes home energy storage, sold through certified installers in nine European countries:

    Product What it is
    Vault 10 A wall-mounted home battery: 10.2 kWh, lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells, 98 kg
    Vault 15 The larger, floor-mounted model: 15.4 kWh, 141 kg
    Bridge A 10 kW hybrid inverter that connects solar panels, Vault batteries and the grid
    Lumora Home The companion app for owners: live energy data, operating modes, warranty claims

    Around the products sit the documents any company has: a 12-year warranty, a returns policy, error codes such as E-204 and E-317, installer certification rules, a safety protocol, and staff policies that a customer assistant should never quote.

    The job: build Lumora's support assistant

    Lumora wants an assistant that answers customer questions correctly, in Lumora's friendly voice, and that never gives unsafe advice about a 10 kWh battery. Ask a general model today and it either guesses or says it doesn't know:

    A customer asks The right answer What a general model typically does
    What's the usable capacity of the Vault 10? 10.2 kWh Says it has no information, or guesses
    How long is the Vault warranty? 12 years Guesses a typical number
    What does error E-204 mean? A communication fault between the Vault and the Bridge Invents something plausible
    There's a burning smell from my battery. What do I do? Keep away, leave the building and call 112, then contact Lumora Support Lists possible causes to investigate

    The last row is the dangerous one. A wrong warranty answer is embarrassing; a confident DIY answer about a smouldering battery is a safety incident.

    What you'll do, module by module

    Each module solves the next problem on the way to that assistant, and each one builds on the previous module's output.

    flowchart LR
  A["Lumora's raw<br/>documents"] -->|"M1 curate"| B["Clean corpus<br/>+ Q&A pairs"]
  B -->|"M2 measure"| C["Test set<br/>+ trusted judge"]
  C -->|"M3 teach"| D["Model that knows<br/>Lumora"]
  D -->|"M4 shrink"| E["Smaller,<br/>faster model"]
  E -->|"M5 make safe"| F["Safety-aligned<br/>assistant"]

    1. Curate Lumora's data

    Lumora's problem: the documents are a mess. The 50-document dump from its website, wiki and support system contains HTML, copies of the same pages, junk pages, spam, pages in three other languages, and support tickets with customers' names and phone numbers.

    You'll: clean, filter and deduplicate it, redact the personal data, then use an LLM to turn the documents into question-answer pairs, keeping only the pairs that are faithful to the source.

    You'll end with: a clean corpus of 26 documents (half the dump discarded) and about 70 grounded Q&A pairs.

    2. Measure what the model knows

    Lumora's problem: before teaching the model anything, you need a fair way to tell whether it worked. "It seems better" isn't evidence.

    You'll: build a held-out test of 30 Lumora questions, score answers with an LLM judge, and calibrate that judge against human labels so it can be trusted. You'll also run a standard general-knowledge benchmark (MMLU), compare models head to head with Elo ratings, and track every run in MLflow.

    You'll see: an uncalibrated judge accepted 21% of hallucinated answers as correct; after calibration it accepted 3%.

    3. Teach the model about Lumora

    Lumora's problem: the base model doesn't know the facts, doesn't answer in a helpful question-and-answer format, and doesn't sound like Lumora.

    You'll: use three techniques, each for a different job:

    • Continued pretraining on the curated documents, to teach the facts
    • Supervised fine-tuning on the Q&A pairs, to teach it to answer questions
    • Preference alignment (DPO), to give it Lumora's warm, casual voice

    You'll see: on the 30 held-out questions, the base model answered 10% correctly; after continued pretraining and fine-tuning, 70%. Its general knowledge (MMLU) stayed level.

    4. Make it small enough to deploy

    Lumora's problem: the assistant has to run cheaply and quickly for thousands of customers.

    You'll: shrink the model with quantization (fewer bits per weight), pruning (removing whole layers) and distillation (a smaller model learning from the larger one), then measure size, speed and accuracy to choose the right trade-off.

    You'll see: 8-bit quantization halves the size with almost no loss; 4-bit loses more unless you quantize in small groups; pruning a quarter of the layers hurts badly, and distillation wins back about two-thirds of the damage.

    5. Assessment: make it safe

    Lumora's problem: when a customer reports smoke, swelling or a hissing battery, or asks how to open the Vault, the assistant must follow Lumora's safety protocol every time, without becoming so cautious that it refuses ordinary questions.

    You'll: build your own preference dataset, align the model to the protocol with DPO, and prove it on hidden prompts you've never seen. An auto-grader scores your work, and passing learners get a completion page.

    You'll see: in our reference run, the base model followed the protocol on 0% of hidden hazard prompts and the aligned model on 80%, with no false alarms on everyday questions.

    About the numbers

    The results above come from our own reference runs on the free-track models (Qwen2.5, 0.5B-1.5B parameters). Yours will differ a little, and that's expected: each module explains what to look for.

    How each module works

    1. Open the module page and its lab side by side. The page explains the ideas; the Open in Colab button opens the matching notebook.
    2. Work through the exercises. Each one ends with an auto-check cell; run it until every line says PASS. Stuck? Every notebook has a USE_SOLUTIONS switch.
    3. Take the quiz at the end of the module page.

    Plan on about 75-85 minutes per module, and 2-3 hours for the assessment.
